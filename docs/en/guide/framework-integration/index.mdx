# Bringing Your Framework to Lynx

Lynx renders your UI with two JavaScript threads: a **main thread** that owns the
pixels, and a **background thread** that owns your app. That one design decision is why
Lynx apps stay responsive under load — and it is also the single fact that makes
integrating a UI framework onto Lynx different from porting it to another browser.

This series is written for framework authors — JavaScript frameworks and
compile-to-JS languages alike. It distills the experience of several real
integrations: **ReactLynx** (the official framework), **Vue Lynx** (vdom and vapor,
with a measured matrix of architecture variants), **Octane** (a compiled
React-model framework), and **miso-lynx** (a Haskell, Elm-architecture framework).
Case-study boxes throughout name their sources.

The good news: the integration surface is layered, and you can stop at any layer with
something real. The series is organized as three stages:

| Stage | Question you're answering | You're done when |
|---|---|---|
| [1 · Build a Bundle](./build-a-bundle.mdx) | How do my sources become a Lynx bundle the engine loads? | Your hello world renders in Lynx Explorer |
| [2 · Drive the UI](./drive-the-ui.mdx) | How does my framework's DOM layer speak the Element PAPI? | Your components render, update, and handle events |
| [3 · Think in Two Threads](./think-in-two-threads.mdx) | Where does my framework *run*, and what crosses the wire? | You've chosen a threading posture on purpose — and measured it |

Stages 1 and 2 are largely mechanical — a few days each with the right map. Stage 3 is
an open architectural space. We know some of its laws (this series states three of
them, with measurements behind each), several of its traps (we fell into them so you
don't have to), and we genuinely do not know its ceiling. That's the invitation: the
second half of stage 3 is yours to explore — and what you learn there transfers back
to the web, because browsers have had idle worker threads waiting for multi-threaded
UI frameworks for a decade.

## The cast, for reference throughout

- **ReactLynx** — compiled JSX "snapshots", dual-slice bundles, always-on instant
  first-frame rendering, tree-adoption handover. The most complete answer today.
- **Vue Lynx** — Vue 3 custom renderer on the background thread, a flat numeric op
  stream, and a measured matrix of template/threading variants.
- **Octane** — React's programming model, compiled; a protocol-first transport whose
  early guard-policy mistakes (and their fixes) are the best documented cautionary
  tale in this series.
- **miso-lynx** — Haskell + the Elm architecture, currently running *entirely on the
  main thread* through a 105-line adapter: proof of how small stages 1–2 can be, and a
  live case study of the fused threading posture.
